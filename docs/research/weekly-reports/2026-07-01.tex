\begin{abstract}
This week I investigated whether nonsmooth neural networks can serve as
continuous-time supermartingale certificates.  The main theoretical outcome is
an It\^o--Tanaka characterization for one-dimensional piecewise-smooth
certificates and a failure result showing that piecewise-linear certificates
cannot solve an important class of reach--avoid problems.  Curvature removes
this obstruction, which motivates either piecewise-quadratic certificates or a
more simply, a network with a bunch of ReLU layers with a piecewise-quadratic in the end.
I also implemented the first verifier scaffold for one-dimensional ReLU certificates and tested it
on an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck and a simple Brownian motion example.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem setting}

Consider a one-dimensional stochastic differential equation
\begin{equation}
  dX_t=f(X_t)\,dt+g(X_t)\,dW_t
  \label{eq:sde}
\end{equation}
on a domain $D$.  For a reach--avoid problem, let $X_0\subseteq D$ be the
initial set, $X_u\subseteq D$ the unsafe set, and $X_\tau\subseteq D$ the target.
We seek a certificate $V$ and constants $\alpha<\beta$ and $\varepsilon>0$
such that
\begin{enumerate}
  \item boundary condition holds: 
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:boundary-conditions}
    \sup_{x\in X_0}V(x)\leq\alpha \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \inf_{x\in X_u}V(x)\geq\beta
  \end{equation}
  \item generator condition holds: 
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:generator-conditions}
    \mathcal LV(x)\leq-\varepsilon \qquad\text{for }x\in\{V\leq\beta\}\setminus X_\tau,
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
where, at smooth points,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal LV(x)=V'(x)f(x)+\frac12V''(x)g(x)^2.
  \label{eq:generator}
\end{equation}
The process is stopped after reaching the target or unsafe set: once the
reach--avoid property has been achieved or violated, its later trajectory is
irrelevant.

In short, these conditions ensure that $V(X_t)$ is a supermartingale (i.e., a decreasing process) that starts below $\alpha$ and is guaranteed to exceed $\beta$ if the process reaches the unsafe
set.

Previously we would assume $V\in\mathcal C^2$.  ReLU networks are
instead continuous and piecewise linear.  Their second derivative vanishes
inside each linear region and is singular at a kink, so the usual It\^o formula
does not directly capture their behaviour.  This is the reason for studying
Tanaka certificates.

\section{Goal of the project}

The first goal of this project is to determine whether nonsmooth verifiers 
(such as ReLU networks which potentially enable ultra-fast verification, or \textbf{even more interestingly: multiple certificates composed together  
using $\max$ and $\min$})
can be used to solve reach--avoid problems.  

Therefore my first task was very simple: analyze the one-dimensional case and determine whether piecewise-linear certificates are expressive enough to solve reach--avoid problems.  

Within this scope, the verifier
should check the two boundary conditions in
\eqref{eq:boundary-conditions}, the decrease condition on the sub-$\beta$
basin outside the target, and the additional conditions introduced at kinks.

\section{Preliminary investigation}

\subsection{The It\^o--Tanaka decomposition}

Let $V$ be continuous and piecewise $\mathcal C^2$, with finitely many
breakpoints $x_i$.  The It\^o--Tanaka formula gives
\begin{align}
  V(X_t)=V(X_0)
  &+\int_0^t \mathcal LV(X_s)\,ds
  +\int_0^t V'(X_s)g(X_s)\,dW_s \notag\\
  &+\frac12\sum_i
  \bigl(V'_+(x_i)-V'_-(x_i)\bigr)L_t^{x_i}(X),
  \label{eq:tanaka}
\end{align}
where $L_t^{x_i}(X)$ is local time.  Subject to the usual integrability
condition making the stochastic integral a martingale, two sign conditions are
sufficient for $V(X_t)$ to be a supermartingale:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal LV(x)&\leq0 &&\text{inside every smooth piece},
  \label{eq:interior}\\
  V'_+(x_i)-V'_-(x_i)&\leq0 &&\text{at every relevant kink}.
  \label{eq:kink}
\end{align}
For a piecewise-linear $V$, condition \eqref{eq:kink} says that
the slopes may only decrease: $V$ must be concave on the region where the
certificate condition is imposed; Figure~\ref{fig:concavity-condition}
illustrates a valid and an invalid sequence of slopes.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{../log/figures/concavity_condition.pdf}
  \caption{The Tanaka kink condition for a continuous piecewise-linear
  certificate.  Decreasing slopes give nonpositive derivative jumps and hence
  a concave certificate (left).  An increase in slope creates a positive
  derivative jump and violates the condition (right).}
  \label{fig:concavity-condition}
\end{figure}

Meanwhile $V''=0$ inside every piece, so
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal LV(x)=V'(x)f(x).
  \label{eq:pl-generator}
\end{equation}

The fact that $V''=0$ inside every linear piece yields a problem, which is illustrated in the next subsection.

\subsection{A motivating counterexample}

For
\begin{equation}
  dX_t=dt+\sqrt2\,dW_t,
  \qquad x\in[0,1],
  \label{eq:constant-sde}
\end{equation}
suppose the boundary requirements are $V(0)=0$ and $V(1)=1$.  Any continuous
piecewise-linear function satisfying them must have positive slope somewhere.
At such a point, \eqref{eq:pl-generator} is positive, so the generator condition
fails.  In contrast, the normalized quadratic candidate
\begin{equation}
  V_{\mathrm{quad}}(x)=2x-x^2
\end{equation}
satisfies the same boundary conditions and
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal LV_{\mathrm{quad}}(x)=V'_{\mathrm{quad}}(x)
  +V''_{\mathrm{quad}}(x)=-2x\leq0.
\end{equation}
Thus the diffusion term can use negative curvature to compensate for positive
drift, while a linear piece cannot.  Figure~\ref{fig:motivating-counterexample}
compares a concave piecewise-linear candidate, the quadratic candidate, and
the exact martingale certificate under identical boundary conditions.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../log/figures/motivating_counterexample.pdf}
  \caption{The motivating counterexample with all candidates normalized by
  $V(0)=0$ and $V(1)=1$.  Even a concave piecewise-linear candidate has a
  positive generator on each affine piece.  Negative curvature makes the
  quadratic generator nonpositive, while the exact certificate has zero
  generator.}
  \label{fig:motivating-counterexample}
\end{figure}

The exact martingale certificate for this example solves
$V'+V''=0$.  With $V(0)=0$ and $V(1)=1$, it is
\begin{equation}
  V_\star(x)=\frac{1-e^{-x}}{1-e^{-1}}.
  \label{eq:exact-certificate}
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
  1-e^{-x}=x-\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{x^3}{6}-\cdots,
\end{equation}
the normalized quadratic candidate is the correspondingly normalized
second-order Taylor approximation of the exact martingale certificate.  This
gives a constructive
interpretation of why quadratic certificates are useful, but does not claim
that they are necessary.

\section{First theoretical results}

\subsection{A class for which piecewise-linear certificates fail}

\paragraph{Theorem (one-sided failure).}
Let \eqref{eq:sde} be stopped at the boundary of $[a,b]$, with
$g(x)^2>0$ in $(a,b)$.  Suppose that positive drift occurs arbitrarily close
to the left boundary:
\begin{equation}
  \text{for every }\delta>0\text{ there is }x\in(a,a+\delta)
  \text{ such that }f(x)>0.
  \label{eq:positive-drift}
\end{equation}
There is no continuous, piecewise-linear certificate $V$ that simultaneously
satisfies $V(a)<V(b)$, the kink condition \eqref{eq:kink}, and the
supermartingale condition on all interior linear regions.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The kink condition makes the slopes of $V$ nonincreasing from left to right.
If the slope on the first linear region were nonpositive, every later slope
would also be nonpositive, contradicting $V(a)<V(b)$.  Hence the first slope
$p$ is positive.  By \eqref{eq:positive-drift}, that region contains a point
$x^\ast$ with $f(x^\ast)>0$.  At this smooth point,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal LV(x^\ast)=p f(x^\ast)>0,
\end{equation}
contradicting the necessary interior condition. \hfill$\square$

There is a symmetric result for a decreasing boundary requirement and negative
drift near the right boundary.  The theorem also clarifies that failure is not
universal: piecewise-linear certificates can work when their slopes have the
opposite sign to the drift.  For example, for the deterministic
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck dynamics $dX_t=-X_t\,dt$, $V(x)=|x|$ has
$\mathcal LV=-|x|\leq0$ away from its kink.

\subsection{Sufficiency for piecewise-quadratic certificates}

Let $V$ be continuous and piecewise quadratic.  If
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\mathcal LV\leq0$ in the interior of every quadratic piece,
  \item $V'_+(x_i)-V'_-(x_i)\leq0$ at every breakpoint, and
  \item $\int_0^tV'(X_s)g(X_s)\,dW_s$ is a martingale,
\end{enumerate}
then \eqref{eq:tanaka} immediately shows that $V(X_t)$ is a supermartingale.
This is a sufficient condition for a proposed certificate, not a claim that a
piecewise-quadratic certificate exists for every SDE or that quadratic degree
is necessary.

An unrestricted deep network with quadratic activations would produce
high-degree polynomial pieces and make verification expensive.  A more
promising architecture is therefore a ReLU network whose final layer is
smooth.  Most of the feature extraction would remain piecewise linear and
cheap, while the certificate output would possess the curvature needed in
examples such as \eqref{eq:constant-sde}.

\section{Verifier scaffold}

I implemented a verifier for ReLU Neural Network certificates for
one-dimensional SDEs.  It currently:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item directly extracts the "pieces" of a ReLU neural network;
  \item checks $\inf_{X_u}V\geq\beta$ and
        $\sup_{X_0}V\leq\alpha$ over unions of intervals;
  \item restricts verification to the sub-$\beta$ basin outside the target;
  \item checks $V'f\leq-\varepsilon$ on every relevant smooth segment; and
  \item checks the concativity Tanaka condition at every relevant kink.
\end{enumerate}

I currently also assume that $f(x)$ is a linear function. I use this assumption 
because I need to check $\sup_{x \in [l, u]} \mathcal{L}V(x) = \sup_{x \in [l, u]} a_i f(x)$.
Perhaps the user would also supply a method \texttt{OneDimensionalSDE.drift\_bounds(lower, upper) -> (lower, upper)} t
hat returns the bounds of $f(x)$ on the interval $[\texttt{lower}, \texttt{upper}]$.
