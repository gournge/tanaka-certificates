\postersection{Introduction}
We study stochastic differential equations
\[
 dX_t=f(X_t)\,dt+\sigma(X_t)\,dW_t,
 \qquad X_t\in K\subset\mathbb R^d,
\]
on compact $K$. Given initial, target, and unsafe sets $X_0,X_T,X_u$, choose
$0\leq\alpha<\beta$ and a \emph{nonnegative} $V:K\to\mathbb R$ satisfying
\[
 \sup_{X_0}V\leq\alpha,
 \quad \inf_{X_u}V\geq\beta,
 \quad \inf_{\partial K\setminus X_T}V\geq\beta.
\]
Let $\tau$ stop the process on reaching $X_T$, $\partial K$, or
$\{V\geq\beta\}$.

\keyeq{\textbf{Theorem (reach--avoid guarantee).} If
$V(X_{t\wedge\tau})$ is a supermartingale, then
\[
 \mathbb P(\tau_{X_u}\wedge\tau_{\partial K}<\tau_{X_T})
 \leq \alpha/\beta.
\]
The proof is the maximal inequality for the nonnegative stopped process.}

\textbf{Following the motivations given above, the following research
questions arise:}\par
\vspace{.7mm}

\researchq{1}{Can non-$C^2$ certificates be valid?}
\textbf{Finding:} Yes. The
It\^o--Tanaka--Meyer formula accounts for kinks and the infinitesimal time
spent at them.
\researchq{2}{What is a useful class of certificates?}
\textbf{Finding:} We
identify three important properties: piecewise-quadratic structure,
representational power, and local-time safety by construction through our
\textbf{Deep Residual ICNN}.
\researchq{3}{Do these certificates work in practice?}
\textbf{Finding:} Yes,
but so far only in very simplified settings. We identify useful techniques
and several failure modes.

\postersection{1. Validity of non-$C^2$ certificates}
Let $V$ be continuous and $C^2$ on each polyhedral cell $K_i$. Across a shared
facet $F$, orient $n_{i\to j,F}$ from $K_i$ to $K_j$ and define the
normal-gradient jump
\[
 \gamma_F(x)=\bigl(\nabla V_j(x)-\nabla V_i(x)\bigr)^\top n_{i\to j,F}.
\]
The multidimensional It\^o--Tanaka formula is
\begin{align*}
 V(X_{t\wedge\tau})={}&V(X_0)
 +\int_0^{t\wedge\tau}\mathcal LV(X_s)\,ds+M_t\\[-1mm]
 &+\frac12\sum_F\int_0^{t\wedge\tau}\gamma_F(X_s)\,dL_s^F,
\end{align*}
where
\[
 \mathcal LV=\nabla V^\top f+\tfrac12
 \operatorname{tr}(\sigma\sigma^\top\nabla^2V),
\]
and $L^F$ is nondecreasing surface local time.

\begin{theorembox}
\textbf{Theorem (It\^o--Tanaka certificate).}
Assume the set constraints above, the stochastic integral $M_t$ is a true
martingale, and below level $\beta$ outside $X_T$:
\[
 \mathcal LV_i(x)\leq0,\qquad \gamma_F(x)\leq0
\]
on every cell and every shared facet. Then $V(X_{t\wedge\tau})$ is a
supermartingale and the reach--avoid bound holds.
\end{theorembox}

\textbf{Proof sketch.} For $s\leq t$, apply the formula on $(s,t]$ and
condition on $\mathcal F_s$. The generator integral is nonpositive;
$dL^F\geq0$ and $\gamma_F\leq0$ make every interface integral nonpositive;
the martingale increment has conditional mean zero. Hence
\[
 \mathbb E[V(X_{t\wedge\tau})\mid\mathcal F_s]
 \leq V(X_{s\wedge\tau}).\qquad\square
\]

\postersection{2. A useful class of non-$C^2$ certificates}
\postersubsection{2.1 Piecewise-quadratic structure}
We parameterize
\[
 V(x)=\sum_{k=1}^m\lambda_k\phi(z_k(x))+c,
 \quad z(x)=\texttt{ReLUNN}(x),
\]
where $\phi$ is a fixed continuous piecewise-quadratic activation. The ReLU
network partitions $K$ into polyhedral cells and, after refinement by $\phi$,
\[
 V_i(x)=c_i+p_i^\top x+\tfrac12x^\top Q_i x.
\]
Thus $\nabla V_i=p_i+Q_ix$ and $\nabla^2V_i=Q_i$ are explicit, turning the
generator and interface requirements into algebraic checks.

\begin{infobox}
\textbf{Why plain ReLU is insufficient.} For
$dX_t=dt+\sqrt2\,dW_t$ on $[0,1]$, suppose $V(0)<V(1)$. A continuous
piecewise-linear $V$ satisfying $\gamma_F\leq0$ has nonincreasing slopes, but
must begin with some positive slope $a_i$. Inside that piece,
\[
 \mathcal LV=V'+V''=a_i>0,
\]
contradicting the generator condition. Kinks alone cannot repair the drift.
The quadratic $V(x)=2x-x^2$ does:
$\mathcal LV=(2-2x)-2=-2x\leq0$.

\postersubsection{2.2 Representational power}
Complex SDEs can require many quadratic regions. Deep neural networks can
represent these regions compactly and discover their partition during
training. Piecewise quadratics also have strong approximation power: under
strict margins, a smooth 2D certificate can be approximated by valid $C^1$
piecewise-quadratic splines. Following existing committor-function literature,
which uses deep neural networks to train committors~[3,4], we use deep networks
to learn certificate-like functions.

\postersubsection{2.3 Local-time safety by construction}
Checking the local-time condition creates a verification bottleneck. If the
network induces $N$ full-dimensional regions, naively testing all pairs costs
$O(N^2)$, even though only adjacent regions sharing a facet can contribute
local time. Explicit adjacency discovery is already expensive as depth and
width increase.

To guarantee the sign without discovering every facet, we propose the
\textbf{Deep Residual ICNN} architecture
\[
 \boxed{\quad V_\theta(x)=\rho\bigl(S_\theta(x)-C_\psi(x)\bigr),
 \qquad \rho>0.\quad}
\]
The $C^1$ piecewise-quadratic branch may be
\[
 S_\theta(x)=c+p^\top x+\tfrac12x^\top Hx
 +\tfrac12\sum_kv_k\operatorname{ReLU}(a_k^\top x+b_k)^2.
\]
The convex piecewise-affine branch is a deep ReLU ICNN~[2]:
\begin{align*}
 z_1&=\operatorname{ReLU}(U_1x+b_1),\\[-1mm]
 z_\ell&=\operatorname{ReLU}(U_\ell x+b_\ell+W_\ell z_{\ell-1}),\\[-1mm]
 C_\psi(x)&=u^\top x+c+w^\top z_L,
 \qquad W_\ell,w\geq0.
\end{align*}
Nonnegative hidden-to-hidden and output weights make $C_\psi$ convex; ReLU
makes it continuous and piecewise affine. Softplus parameterization enforces
the weight constraints during unconstrained training.

\begin{theorembox}
\textbf{Theorem (local-time condition by construction).}
For every pair of adjacent cells and every parameter choice,
\[
 \bigl(\nabla V_{\theta,j}-\nabla V_{\theta,i}\bigr)^\top
 n_{i\to j,F}\leq0.
\]
Therefore all codimension-one local-time contributions are nonpositive; no
activation-region enumeration is required to establish their sign.
\end{theorembox}

\textbf{Proof sketch for §2.3.} Restrict convex $C_\psi$ to a line normal to
$F$. The derivative of a one-dimensional convex function is nondecreasing, so
\[
 (\nabla C_{\psi,j}-\nabla C_{\psi,i})^\top n_{i\to j,F}\geq0.
\]
Because $S_\theta\in C^1$, its gradient jump is zero. Consequently
\begin{align*}
 (\nabla V_{\theta,j}-\nabla V_{\theta,i})^\top n
 &=-\rho(\nabla C_{\psi,j}-\nabla C_{\psi,i})^\top n\\[-1mm]
 &\leq0.\qquad\square
\end{align*}
This controls only interface curvature. Nonnegativity, set separation, the
interior generator inequality, and integrability must still be verified.

\postersection{3. Experiments and failure modes}
At first, we tried to train a plain ReLU MLP with a piecewise-quadratic output
layer and found that it was very difficult to train.

After proposing the Deep Residual ICNN architecture, training became easier
and we found valid certificates for a simple SDE with loose bounds.

\postersection{Limitations and future work}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We could not train a valid certificate using Adam alone; success
required linear programming on the $S_{\theta_1}$ branch.
\item The ICNN branch $C_{\theta_2}$ was almost zero and contributed little to
the final certificate.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{We found a connection to a large body of literature on committor
functions, which offers efficient methods for training certificate-like
functions~[3,4]. Its sampling methods improved our training with Adam,
although they have not yet produced a verified certificate.}

These results remain limited to simplified settings. The main open challenge
is to make both branches train effectively while retaining exact verification.

\postersection{Preliminaries and notation}
\textbf{Martingales.} On a filtered probability space
$(\Omega,\mathcal F,(\mathcal F_t)_{t\geq0},\mathbb P)$, an adapted integrable
process $Y_t$ is a supermartingale when
\[
 \mathbb E[Y_t\mid\mathcal F_s]\leq Y_s,\qquad s\leq t.
\]
It is a martingale when equality holds.

\textbf{Generator.} For the SDE above,
\[
 \mathcal LV=\nabla V^\top f+
 \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\sigma^\top\nabla^2V).
\]
The first term is the Lie derivative along the deterministic drift; the
second records curvature sampled by the diffusion.

\textbf{Local time.} $L_t^F$ measures the infinitesimal time accumulated by
$X_t$ at a kink facet $F$. Its contribution is weighted by the signed
normal-gradient jump $\gamma_F$.

% \textbf{Input-convex neural network.} An ICNN is convex in its input because
% its recurrent and output weights are nonnegative and its activations are
% convex and nondecreasing. We subtract this convex branch so its gradient jumps
% have the supermartingale sign.

\postersection{Python package}
We propose a Python package, \texttt{tanaka\_certificates}, with utilities for
verifying Tanaka certificates and a thorough test suite. Example usage:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=posterpython,
  caption={Verify a piecewise-quadratic certificate.},captionpos=b]
# pip install tanaka-certificates

sde, problem = make_piecewise_quadratic_ou_2d_problem()
verifier = VerifierPiecewiseQuadratic(
    sde, problem, _ou_example_certificate())

assert verifier.verify() is VerificationResult.NOT_VERIFIED
issue = next(i for i in verifier.issues
    if i.kind is IssueKind.DOMAIN_BOUNDARY)
assert issue.value < problem.beta
\end{lstlisting}
